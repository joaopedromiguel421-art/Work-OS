# Positioning and offer

## Inputs

Require a decision objective plus the best available customer, product, market, cost, and proof evidence. If material evidence is missing, invoke `market-intelligence` or `product-discovery` first.

## Method

1. Select a specific target segment and high-priority situation/job. State who is deliberately not targeted.
2. Describe current alternatives and the criteria customers use to choose.
3. Identify differentiated capabilities and translate them into outcomes, with proof and caveats.
4. Choose a category/reference frame that makes the product understandable without hiding meaningful differences.
5. Write a positioning statement: target, problem/context, category, primary value, differentiated reason, and proof.
6. Design the offer: scope, deliverables, time-to-value, packaging, price hypothesis/range, risk reversal where truthful, qualification, constraints, and next action.
7. Test internal coherence against unit economics, delivery capacity, brand, compliance, and unsupported-claim risk.
8. Define evidence or experiments needed before a public pricing or guarantee decision.

## Guardrails

Do not fabricate scarcity, urgency, testimonials, guarantees, comparative superiority, credentials, or quantified outcomes. Pricing, discounts, guarantees, paid commitments, and publication require the appropriate human owner.

## Boundaries

Use `copy-and-content` to express an accepted position, `landing-page` to build a page, `campaign-growth` for distribution, and `sales` for account conversations. This Skill owns the strategic offer contract.

## Output

ICP and exclusions, alternatives, positioning, message pillars/proof, offer/package, pricing assumptions, objections, claim constraints, and validation plan.
