# Product discovery

## Method

1. Frame the decision and product outcome, not a favored solution.
2. Identify primary users, jobs, current alternatives, constraints, and who bears the cost of failure.
3. Inventory evidence: observed behavior, interviews, support themes, analytics, market evidence, technical constraints, and stakeholder assertions. Label assumption and bias.
4. Map problem/opportunity areas and the smallest behavior change that could produce the outcome.
5. Define hypotheses with target segment, intervention, expected behavior, measure, guardrail, and invalidation signal.
6. Rank by evidence, outcome potential, risk reduction, effort range, and reversibility. Do not manufacture precision.
7. Choose the next learning step: prototype, interview, instrumentation, technical spike, experiment, or delivery spec.

## Boundaries

Stay in the main conversation because product constraints and delivery choices are dependent. Use `research-scout` only for an independent evidence gap. Use `market-intelligence` for external category/competitor evidence, `analytics-measurement` for instrumentation, and `spec-delivery` once an outcome is ready for delivery.

Do not turn fictional personas into evidence, treat requests as validated needs, or optimize a metric without user and business guardrails.

## Output

Decision frame; user/problem evidence; assumptions and risks; opportunity options; selected hypothesis; learning plan; success/guardrail measures; criteria to enter delivery.

## Sources and adaptation

Original product procedure informed by research and operations output contracts in Anthropic Knowledge Work Plugins and the evidence-first discipline used across the approved spec sources.
