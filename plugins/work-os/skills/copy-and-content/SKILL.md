# Copy and content

## Method

1. Establish audience, situation, desired action, channel, stage of awareness, voice authority, constraints, and approved proof/claims.
2. Choose one message hierarchy: core promise, problem/context, mechanism, benefits, proof, objection handling, and action as applicable.
3. Draft for comprehension before cleverness. Use concrete language, information scent, and content that matches the user's likely questions.
4. Support claims with project evidence. Mark placeholders that require legal, product, customer, or compliance approval.
5. Adapt structure and length to the channel without changing the underlying claim.
6. Edit for accuracy, specificity, voice consistency, scanability, accessibility, and removal of repetition or false urgency.
7. Provide variants only when they test a meaningful hypothesis, not cosmetic synonyms.

## Content planning

For a plan, map audience questions and business outcomes to topics, formats, distribution, owner, reuse, update cadence, and measurement. Avoid volume targets without a quality/distribution model.

## Guardrails

Never invent testimonials, customer quotes, research, results, certifications, limited availability, or competitor claims. External publishing and outreach require explicit approval.

## Boundaries

Use `positioning-and-offer` to decide the strategic message, `brand-system` for voice rules, `seo` for search intent, `landing-page` for the full page system, and `technical-documentation` for instructional/reference work.

## Output

Brief, message hierarchy, draft, evidence/claim notes, meaningful variants, channel adaptations, and review/approval checklist.
