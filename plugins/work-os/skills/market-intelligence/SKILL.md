# Market intelligence

## Method

1. State the strategic decision, geography, segment, category definition, time horizon, and freshness requirement.
2. Define the comparison frame before researching: target customer, job, product scope, route to market, pricing/packaging, proof, strengths, weaknesses, and strategic signals as relevant.
3. Gather primary evidence from official product/pricing/docs, regulatory filings, first-party announcements, original research, and customer/user evidence. Add independent corroboration for contested claims.
4. Distinguish direct competitors, adjacent alternatives, substitutes, and the status quo.
5. Normalize differences in edition, currency, billing period, region, date, and claimed versus observed capability.
6. Synthesize patterns, whitespace, risks, and implications. A missing public fact stays unknown.

## Separate context

Use `research-scout` for two or more independent competitor/source branches or lengthy documents. It returns a source table; the main conversation integrates the landscape and recommendation.

## Guardrails

Use public, authorized sources. Do not bypass access controls, scrape against terms, collect sensitive personal data, impersonate buyers, or state allegations without strong evidence. Date all volatile pricing and product claims.

## Boundaries

Use `evidence-research` for a non-market question, `positioning-and-offer` to turn findings into a strategic choice, `sales` for account-specific commercial work, and `seo` for search demand.

## Output

Decision frame, landscape map, normalized comparison, evidence table, patterns/contradictions, strategic implications, confidence/expiry, and recommended next validation.
